# Storey: design and technical review, October 2026

Status: review only, nothing built. Three parallel reviews (design, state and server, rendering and editors) read the code at `cf7db41` and the live app in headless Chromium at 1440×900 and 1024×768. The full reports are in `docs/review-2026-10/` (`design.md`, `tech-state.md`, `tech-render.md`); this page is the synthesis and the roadmap. The house is "listing 91770873" throughout.

The brief: make Storey **simple, intuitive and sexy**, and review the engineering.

## 1. Verdict

| | Score | In one line |
|---|---|---|
| Simple | 6/10 | The Studio shell (top bar, five modes, dock, one inspector card) is the right skeleton. Every mode panel is still a developer form. |
| Intuitive | 5/10 | The modes read as a pipeline, which is good, but the first 30 seconds are a generic dialog over a half-hidden empty state, and comparing As listed with Proposed, the product's payoff, is a dropdown in the dock. |
| Sexy | 5/10 | The Review page and the brand tokens are genuinely good. The 3D canvas is not: a lime ground, chalk-white neighbours brighter than the house, and photo pins scattered over the roof. |
| Engineering | strong | Pure logic in `src/lib` with 882 behavioural tests, on-demand rendering, merged meshes, careful auth and sync. The weak spots are the failure paths, a handful of real bugs, a 2,075-line plan editor, and no lint or CI. |

The three changes that move the product most:

1. **A designed first run and home view.** One welcome card with two picture tiles replaces the startup dialog and the empty state; the camera frames the houses, not the plots, and sits clear of the inspector.
2. **An inspector kit.** Designed controls (finish tiles with real texture thumbnails, swatch rows, paired W × D fields, a danger zone, a stat header) replace native `<select>` and `<input type=color>` in every mode. Decorate first.
3. **A quieter canvas and plan.** Matte grey neighbours, a ground that fades into the page, pins off by default; on the plan, a faint floorplan image, handles only on the selected room, teal selection, an architectural stair symbol.

## 3. UX, UI and design

### First run
- Today: a 380 px `choose()` dialog ("Load As listed" / "Load As listed and the proposed extension" / "Not now") over an empty state whose teal "Open layouts" button pokes out under the dialog. The top bar is fully drawn but disabled, so the app looks broken.
- Proposal: one welcome card with the outline mark, "Listing 91770873", one line of purpose, two picture tiles ("The house as listed · 2 floors · 118 m²" and "With my planned extension · 146 m²"), and "Start empty" as a quiet link. Then a one-time three-step coach strip above the dock. Hide the layout switch, undo/redo and palette until a house exists.
- Add a one-line subtitle per mode under the card title (Build: "Rooms, walls, doors and stairs"; Decorate: "Floors, walls and colours"; …).

### Information architecture
- **"View" sits next to "Views"** in the dock. Rename them "Show" (layers) and "Cameras" (saved views).
- **Three furniture entry points** ("Add furniture", "Add a piece I own" twice, "Place furniture on the plan"). Make it one "Add furniture"; in 3D it switches to Plan and opens the picker.
- **Compare is buried** as "Compare: Single" in the dock. Make the layout switch three-way: `As listed | Proposed | ⇆ Compare`, Wipe by default, Side by side and Overlay as a small in-canvas segment.
- **Plan/3D** is the biggest view change in the app and the smallest control. Move it to the top bar beside the house name; the dock becomes floors · sun · Show · Cameras.
- **"Plot" hides the roof** (Exterior lives under it). Rename the mode "Outside", or move Exterior into Build.
- The Plot card stacks three headings ("Plot", "Site plan", "Site") before its first control.
- Stairs are a thing on the plan, so select them by clicking the symbol, not from "Floor settings".
- The house menu mixes project, settings and about; move Render quality, Look and Clear all into a Settings modal.
- Six pill groups share one screen in 3D. Keep three: the mode bar as underline tabs, the layout switch, the dock.

### Inspector panels
- Build leads with "Lock all rooms", a protective action, where the first-timer needs "Click a room to check its size". "Level 0 · 73.0 px/m" is internal calibration; show "Ground floor". The orange warning paragraph restates the numbers above it; make it a chip: "10% narrower than printed · Check scale".
- Decorate is the mode that should feel like a design app and feels most like a form. Finish `<select>`s become 56 px tile grids drawn from the procedural textures; the 250 px-wide colour wells become a swatch row plus a round custom well and a reset icon; "Pattern direction (degrees)" becomes a segmented control. Today the quick colours sit in a separate block under all the wells, so the user has to work out which palette feeds which.
- Furnish's empty-state copy is good; the "Shift+click adds items…" tip is power-user help shown to everyone. The catalogue popover is the best-designed control in the app; give rows a plan-symbol thumbnail.
- Plot: the yellow note on every visit, the view toggles that belong in "Show", "on boundary" wrapping in the distance cards, neighbours as rows rather than three fields each.
- Standardise destructive actions as a danger zone (ghost red button after a hairline), never a grey-filled red-text button beside neutral grey buttons.
- The microcopy table in `design.md` §8 lists about 30 label rewrites.

### Plan editor
- Double labels: Storey's room labels sit on top of the printed floorplan's own labels. Default the image to faint, offer Off / Faint / Trace, and hide Storey's labels while tracing.
- Six hues with no legend (floor-finish tints, red garage door, violet stairs, cyan canopy, orange selection, blue handles). Neutral room fills in Build, finish colours only in Decorate, teal selection (orange is the warning colour), handles only on the selected room, horizontal dimension chips, an architectural stair symbol.
- The icon-only rail needs instant labelled flyouts ("Door  D"); the polygon, wall and gap glyphs are the weakest.
- "Fit" frames the plan under the inspector card. Fit to the visible rectangle.
- The right-click menu is excellent and undiscoverable; hint it once.

### 3D view
- Home view: the house fills about 30% of the frame, the inspector covers the right neighbour, and two white blocks dominate. Frame the union of both layouts' houses (not plots) with a 15% margin, offset for the card, pitch down less. This changes studio-plan §1.6.
- Neighbours: mid warm grey, matte, so the house is the only textured object. Ground: fade to `--bg`, tone the green down.
- Photo pins: off by default (a "Photos" toggle in Show), camera icons at floor level for the floor in view, one pin per side for exterior photos.
- The overlay legend is a sentence in a pill; make it a legend with dots. Red/green is the classic colour-vision pair; use teal and a hatched warm red.
- In side by side and wipe, auto-collapse the inspector card so it does not cover the extension.
- The sun icon in the dock should turn the sun on; the checkbox goes.

### Visual system
- Bricolage Grotesque only at 18 px and up; at 14–15 px it reads as a wobbly Plex. Card titles become 12 px all-caps Plex, as the Review tiles already do.
- Tokens are good but leak: raw hex in `.note` and disabled text, 54 raw hex colours in `PlanEditor.tsx` and 188 outside `styles.css` overall. Add `--sel`, `--plan-*`, `--note-*`, `--disabled`.
- `--muted #6a736f` on `--bg #eef0ec` is about 4.3:1 and fails AA; darken to about `#5f6864`. The input focus ring (`--accent-soft`) is nearly invisible.
- Studio vs Glass: neither is clearly sexier. Glass drops the teal accent and loses legibility over the plan. Recommend one look: Studio's solid cards and teal plus Glass's full-bleed canvas and floating top bar in 3D only, and remove the Look setting. This changes studio-plan §1.15 and 2C.
- Minimum supported width 1180 px, designed for 1280 and up, with a stepped top bar (wordmark to mark under 1360, compact layout switch under 1240, switch into the house menu under 1180).

### Accessibility
- Modal has no focus trap, no focus return and no inert background; three different dialog implementations exist. Native `<dialog>` with `showModal()` would fix the trap, inert and z-order at once.
- Segmented buttons use `.active` without `aria-pressed`; the dock claims `role="toolbar"` without arrow keys; the palette input has no label or `aria-activedescendant`; the active rail tool is not announced.
- The plan SVG and the 3D canvas are mouse-only. A roving tab index over rooms and items is the cheap fix for the plan.
- Hit targets of 14–24 px (rail chevron, anchor arrows, info buttons, "Clear" and "remove" links).

## 4. Technical

### What is good
- `src/lib` is pure and tested: 71 files, 882 tests, behavioural rather than snapshot, named as sentences. Zero TODOs. Comments explain intent, consistently.
- The 3D scene: `frameloop="demand"` (0 frames in 3 s idle), one merged mesh per material per floor rebuilt only on structural change (8 ms for the ground floor), shadow map redrawn only on change, PMREM throttled, post-processing lazy and only on High, geometry disposed. Hover is isolated (no house re-render).
- Server: timing-safe passcode compare, HMAC tokens keyed on secret and passcode, HttpOnly/SameSite/Secure cookie, strict redirect and traversal guards, Origin check on PUT, atomic tmp-and-rename writes, immutable hashed assets.
- Sync: a conservative revision protocol that never overwrites silently; the pure decision logic is tested.
- Privacy: no address, postcode, street name, title number or owner name anywhere in the repo. The 37 bundled photos and the floorplan carry no EXIF, GPS or XMP. All user text in the report HTML and plan SVG exports is escaped; CSV cells are protected against formula injection. `npm audit --omit=dev` reports 0 vulnerabilities (4 dev-only ones, fixed by `npm audit fix`).

### Where it is fragile
- **Failure paths** (B4, B5, B7, B9): the app has no recovery screen and no notion of a failed save.
- **Data loss paths** (B5, B6): plus the server keeps no backups of `project.json`, and an unreadable file at start-up is ignored and then overwritten by the next upload.
- **Sync payloads**: every save uploads the whole project with photos embedded as base64 (the listing photos alone are about 5 MB); the server buffers up to 50 MB and parses it synchronously, so two concurrent saves could exhaust a 512 MB instance. The fix is content-addressed image upload (`PUT /api/images/<sha256>`), reusing the `@@img:` references `persist.ts` already uses locally.
- **Plan editor**: one 2,075-line component with 26 `useState`s. Each pointermove writes the store synchronously: 2 React commits and 10 SVG mutations per move (one commit is the hidden 3D view, B8). Undo granularity is correct (one step per drag). Proposed split: `usePlanDrag`, `usePlanKeys`, `usePlanSelection`, `usePlanTools`, memoised layer components, rAF-coalesced writes, a CSS transform during pan. `SiteEditor.tsx` (957 lines) should share the drag and camera hooks.
- **Compare modes** mount separate WebGL contexts per mode and tear them down on every switch, recompiling shaders and re-uploading about 70 textures each time. Wipe draws two full-size canvases and clips one with CSS. Long term: one renderer with scissored views (drei `<View>`); first step: keep both viewers mounted across side-by-side and wipe.
- **High quality** doubles draw calls (181 to 353) before ambient occlusion runs, because glass `transmission = 1` renders the scene twice. The default-framebuffer MSAA is wasted when the composer runs.
- **Textures**: colour maps are drawn per colour, and the native colour picker fires continuously, so dragging over a terrazzo or herringbone floor costs 70–110 ms per step. Draw patterns once in greys and tint by `material.color`, or debounce the picker.
- **Photo pins** decode the full 1024×768 photo per pin (about 90 MB of VRAM per context) and the photos are held as 4.76 MB of base64 strings in the store. Use 256 px thumbnails for pins.
- **Memoisation contracts**: about 22 `eslint-disable react-hooks/exhaustive-deps` comments, but ESLint is not installed, so nothing checks them.
- **Undo coalescing** on wall-clock time (700 ms): `loadListing`, `applyListingReview` and `applyProject` rely on it to count as one step, and three of `applyProject`'s writes bypass history entirely. Add `transact(fn)`.
- **Model**: `Opening` is a bag of 15 optional fields scoped by comment; `Floor` carries seven `stairs*` fields and so allows one flight; photos link to rooms by floor and room name, which breaks silently on duplicate names. Exports and server copies carry no format version, so only idempotent fixes can run on import.
- **Tooling**: no lint, no formatter, no CI; `server/`, `scripts/` and `vite.config.ts` are never typechecked; Render deploys `main` with `npm run build` only. The hosted build is an HTML fragment plus 80 asset files, not a single file. Brand colours and the splash SVG are copied into `page.mjs` and `build-hosted.mjs` by hand.
- **Server hardening**: minimum passcode length 4 with a global 60-per-hour lockout (brute-forceable in days, and anyone can block new sign-ins for an hour); no CSP or HSTS; logout is a GET; `project.json` has no backups.
- **Bundle**: shell about 525 kB minified (180 kB gzip); the 3D view adds about 1.07 MB (303 kB gzip); High adds 227 kB. Tree-shaking works. The chunk Vite names "capture" (512 kB) is three's renderer plus react-three-fiber, named after its first importer; add explicit `three` and `r3f` chunk groups. `__BUILD_DATE__` changes the entry hash daily.
- **Large files**: `PlanEditor.tsx` 2,075 (CLAUDE.md says about 1,400), `textures.ts` 1,704, `roof.ts` 1,416, `planning.ts` 1,294 (one 575-line function), `openings.ts` 1,040, `Inspectors.tsx` 1,025, `SiteEditor.tsx` 957, `Fixtures.tsx` 799 (650 lines of catalogue geometry that belongs in lib with tests).

### Decisions for the owner
Four recommendations change decisions recorded in `docs/studio-plan.md` §1 or the modes brief: home framing on houses not plots (§1.6), one look instead of Studio and Glass (§1.15, 2C), a new startup flow (§3.9), and moving Plan/3D and Compare with "Plot" renamed "Outside" (§3.2, §3.5, label rule). Everything else can proceed without reopening a decision.

One privacy note: the code names the property portal beside the listing id in two places and in the placeholder image. The id plus the portal is a lookup key for the public listing. The privacy rule allows the id; the owner should decide whether the portal's name stays.
