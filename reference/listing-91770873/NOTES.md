# Listing 91770873: reference notes

Facts used to build the built-in model (`src/lib/houses/listing91770873.ts`), and where each came from. Assets are in `src/assets/listing-91770873/` (the listing's floorplan and 37 photos).

## The house

- 1928 interwar semi-detached house, joined to No. 26 (the corner plot) on the plan's right (east). Garage side faces the west neighbour.
- Two-storey part: cream render, hipped roof at the garage end, flush at the party wall, concrete interlocking tiles, brown uPVC fascias, gutters and windows. One brick chimney stack on the party wall near the ridge (chimney breasts on that wall on both floors of the plan): flat-topped with no pots, carrying TV aerials, about 1.1 m wide. The roof is shallow, about 28° (a camera fitted to the walls in front-street puts the ridge there); No. 26 shares it. Downpipes: the front gutter's comes down the west side wall at the front corner, beside the bathroom's soil and vent stack, which rises through the eaves to a vent terminal (front-3); the rear gutter's runs down between the patio doors and the kitchen window, with nothing at the west rear corner (rear-1, rear-4); a small pipe drops off the single-storey's flat roof at its west rear corner by the back door (rear-1).
- Single-storey part (garage, galley, front hall): red brick at the front, rendered at the rear. Flat roof, dressed across the whole front (porch, garage and hall) with one tiled slope under one fascia and gutter line; the porch over the drive is under the same roof, on a brick pier at its front-left corner (front-2, front-3, front-street).
  - The slope rises about 0.9 m at about 30°, all the way up to the two-storey wall over the hall, with a thin flashing where it meets it (front-1, front-street). Measured against the garage door in front-street, the fascia's underside is about 2.2 m above the garage floor and the top of the slope about 3.2 m.
  - Where the slope meets the two-storey wall over the hall (front-2, front-street, front-1, measured with the fitted front-2 and front-street cameras): its top is about 3.3 m above the ground floor (3.35–3.38 in front-2, 3.25 in front-street), about 0.2–0.3 m under the first-floor sills. Its gutter is about 2.4 m up and its fascia about 0.2 m deep (bottom about 2.2 m), so the eaves sit on the wall tops, not up at the flat roof's deck; the slope is still about 30° and rises about 0.9 m (28–30° from the gutter to the top over its 1.6 m run). The model sets the skirt's eaves 0.26 m below the deck (`drop`), which puts the gutter at 2.4 m and the top at 3.31 m; level with the deck it reached 3.57 m, with its flashing to 3.67 m.
  - The two front first-floor windows (bathroom over the hall, bedroom 3 over the bay) share one sill and head in every front photo: about 0.9 m and 2.0–2.1 m above the first floor (front-2 reads 0.92–0.99 and 2.01–2.11; bathroom-1 agrees from inside, the window board about at basin height). The bathroom's had been set at 1.2 m, which kept it clear of the too-high skirt; at 0.9 m it would have been cut by it. Both now use 0.9 m, and the slope's flashing tops out about 0.14 m under the sills. The Proposed extension's assumed front window (sill 0.9 m) was cut by the same too-high skirt, by about 0.1 m; it now clears it too.
  - Hipped at both ends, with hip tiles. On the right the hip runs back to the two-storey wall beside the bay, with a downpipe in that corner (front-1, front-2). The left end is hipped over the pier and the gutter turns the corner (front-3); how far the slope returns down the garage side is not visible (it faces the boundary). The model assumes 2 m.
  - The rear photos (rear-1, rear-4) show nothing rising above the plain flat edge at the back, so the slope does not run the length of the garage side.
  - Brown fascia about 0.2 m deep below a brown half-round gutter. Under the porch, the garage's front wall around the door is cream render, not brick (front-3).
- Front door: dark brown with glazed panels, on the side of the front hall projection under the porch canopy. Garage door: red, up-and-over.
- Canted bay on the front living room with a small canopy roof: about 1.6 m wide at the wall, with plain render either side, about 0.7 m to the party wall and 0.9 m to the hall (front-1, front-2, living-front-2). The canopy has a lead-grey top and a shaped brown timber fascia a little wider than the bay (the model's canopy follows the bay outline).
- The 2.0 m opening between the two living rooms is an open, square-headed archway with no doors (not bi-folds), lined and trimmed on both faces in dark stained timber. In living-front-1 its head is about 2.1 m (about 87% of the 2.4 m ceiling) and its overall width, architraves included, about 2.2 m; the model keeps the 2.0 m traced width and a 2.1 m head, drawn as the "archway" opening kind (living-rear-1, living-front-1, living-rear-3).
- Chimney breasts: the plan draws one on the party wall in the rear living room (plan x 1258–1290, y 97–210: about 1.55 m wide, projecting about 0.38 m, an alcove either side) and one in Bedroom 3's north-east corner (x 1050–1075, y 1040–1112, projecting about 0.25 m). Both are notches in the room outlines, with a short wall closing the party wall behind each. The rear living room's gold floral paper covers the breast and both alcoves; it has a carved mahogany surround, marble hearth and back panel and a brass gas fire (living-rear-1, -3). Bedroom 3's breast has no fireplace; it and the party wall are dusky mauve, the other walls a very pale lilac-grey (bedroom-3-1 to -3).
- Kitchen (kitchen-1 to -4, galley-2): cream shaker units with a dark speckled worktop along the whole rear wall from the back door's jamb, with a washing-machine space at the galley/kitchen junction and the sink under the galley window, returning about 1.1 m down the kitchen's east wall. The galley's west wall has a base unit, a black gas hob over a double oven and an under-counter fridge, under three cream wall units, the middle one short over a black canopy hood. The galley units are modelled 0.5 m deep so they stop at the back door's jamb.
- Serving hatch, kitchen to rear living room (kitchen-1, -2, living-rear-2): on the kitchen side a dark timber frame about 1.25 m wide sits over the returned units, but only its south half (about 0.65 m, sill about 1.0 m, head about 1.45 m) is open, glazed with frosted sliding panes; the north half is a papered panel. The living room side shows just that opening. Position fitted to kitchen-1's perspective; the model draws the opening only.
- Two more doors: a glazed dark door across the corridor between the front hall and the kitchen (plan y 443, hung on its east jamb; galley-1, which is the front hall, not the galley), in a short partition, and a dark ledged plank door from the kitchen into the under-stairs cupboard (plan x 836–890 on y 345; kitchen-2). The cupboard stays part of the stairs hall in the model.

## Orientation and plot

Measured from the OS-based map in the drainage and water search (north-up), scaled so the house outline matches the traced model (agreement within about 0.15 m). Map accuracy is roughly ±0.3 m.

- Front faces a compass bearing of about 162° (south-south-east); the garden faces about 342°. The floorplan's own compass says about 171°.
- Plot about 10.6 m wide and 16.7–17.6 m deep (the rear boundary is skewed). The house fills the plot's width: the garage wall is on the west boundary, the party wall on the east.
- Rear garden about 5.8–6.6 m deep; front garden about 2.6 m in front of the hall and 4.3 m in front of the bay.
- The west side of the drive is a brick wall, not a fence: the same red brick and coping as the front garden wall, about 1 m high at the pavement, running back into the brick pier at the garage's front-left corner, which rises out of the end of the wall (the model ends the wall under the porch pillar, its street face flush with the pillar's) (front-1, front-2, front-3). Its brick-on-edge top is level from the pavement for most of its length, then steps up one course (about 75 mm) for the last half metre or so, where the pier rises out of it (clearest in front-2, also front-3 and front-street); it does not rake. Its base follows the drive: the drive is laid against it, so it shows less of the wall as it climbs. The model uses `follow: "step"` with `stepEvery: 2.45` on this edge (a level run of 2.45 m from the pavement, then a one-course step); the step position is judged by eye. Behind the pier the boundary is a timber fence again (front-3). The model splits the west edge at about 4.3 m back from the pavement line.
- The drive rises gently from the pavement to the garage door; there is no step up to the garage (front-3, front-street), only a thin threshold, and the front door's landing under the porch sits on the same slope (a small step at most). The model ramps the drive from ground level at the pavement to 0.24 m at the garage door (about 1 in 18 over 4.3 m), 0.02 m under the ground floor's finished level, which is 0.26 m above the ground, so the front door (about 2 m back from the pavement) has a step of about 0.07 m. A paved strip along the front of the hall, kerbed from the lawn (front-3), carries on from the drive's east edge at the drive's height (0.16 m falling to 0.11 m away from the wall). Both levels are estimates from the photos. With its one-course step, the 1 m drive wall stands about 0.92 m above the drive at the pier.
- The garden walls have no concrete coping and no regular piers: the top is a course of red bricks laid on edge, slightly weathered. The model draws this as `top: "brick"`, `piers: false` on every wall edge. The two rear walls carry a pierced block panel on top that cannot be drawn, so they use the same brick-on-edge top as the closest match.
- Other boundaries (all from the photos; positions along each edge are estimates):

  - East, back garden: a brick wall with a pierced decorative block panel, about 1.4 m, beside No. 26's conservatory (rear-3), modelled from the house's rear corner to the conservatory's far end. The panel itself cannot be drawn (plain wall). Further back the boundary is not visible in any photo (fence kept).
  - East, along the party wall: not visible (fence kept). East, front garden: a low brick wall like the front one, about 0.95 m, from the house front to the pavement (owner; front-garden, front-1).
  - West, at the back of the house: a brick wall about 1.3 m with a decorative block panel along the top and a grey bin store against it (rear-1, rear-2, rear-4), then a hedge, about 2 m, further back. The panel and bin store are not drawn. rear-garden looks east along the house from the west end of the patio, not along the west boundary: the shrub bed and conifer on its left are the raised bed below, which runs parallel to the house, and the conservatory in the distance is No. 26's.
